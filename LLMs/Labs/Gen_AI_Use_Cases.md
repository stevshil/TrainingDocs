# ⏱️ 20‑Minute Group Activity: AI Use Cases & Ethics

This is a group task.  Split yourselves in to groups, your instructor will tell you how many per group.  

Then in your groups you will identify a use case that you are all confident would benefit from **Generative AI**.

Once you have decided on a **use case** you should review whether it would benefit from **Generative AI** or **not** based on the criteria for building AI applications.

At the end of the task you should be able to prove that your **use case** should or shouldn't be AI based.

You might choose to present this information in a table, presentation, or other method, but you must be convincing and reason if required, proving that you have considered the areas essential to creating an AI process.

## 👥 1. Choose a shared topic (3 minutes)

Pick something everyone knows well;
- workflows
- hobbies
- team processes
- daily-life routines

Examples:
- customer support
- training content creation
- project management
- health & fitness tracking

These are only suggestions, we want you to pick something you are all familiar with, or think would make a greate **Gen AI** app.

## 💡 2. Identify GenAI opportunities (5 minutes)

Discuss where GenAI could realistically help in your chosen topic.

To identify suitable use cases for Generative AI, consider the following:
- Content Creation Needs: Tasks requiring new text, images, or media.
- Automation Opportunities: Processes where creative automation boosts efficiency.
- Synthetic Data Requirements: Industries needing data for training or testing.
- Personalization at Scale: Applications like marketing or education needing tailored outputs.
- Human-like Interaction: Scenarios requiring conversational AI, such as chatbots.
- Innovation Potential: Areas where novel solutions can disrupt traditional methods.
- Data-Driven Insights: Enhancing analytics with generated scenarios or forecasts.
- Strategic Importance: Aligning AI capabilities with organizational goals for competitive advantage, with a focus on automation, creativity, and personalization as key drivers in 2025 ([Strategic Importance of GenAI](https://hatchworks.com/blog/generative-ai-use-cases-across-industries/)).

### 📊 Record your insights to each of these

- Identifying which are specific to your AI process, and ignore those that are not.
- How relevant are they to your process
- How will they relate to your AI process

## 🤔 3. Surface ethical considerations (5 minutes)

Generative AI introduces complex ethical challenges that require careful management.

For each use case, identify risks:

- Harmful Content: Risk of generating offensive or dangerous material, such as deepfakes
- Copyright Issues: Potential infringement from training on copyrighted data
- Data Privacy: Handling of sensitive information in training datasets, with increasing regulatory scrutiny like GDPR and CCPA
- Bias Amplification: Perpetuating societal biases in outputs, necessitating advanced mitigation strategies
- Workforce Displacement: Automating tasks traditionally done by humans, raising concerns about reskilling needs
- AI Hallucinations: Generating inaccurate or fabricated content, a growing issue as models scale
- Environmental Impact: High energy consumption of large models, prompting calls for sustainable AI practices
- Misuse of AI-Generated Content: Ethical concerns around AI companions, such as emotional dependency or manipulation, and misuse in trust-based scenarios

Discuss how these risks might appear in your chosen topic. Grade these risks based on severity and how they will impact your design.

## 📝 4. Responsible‑use planning (5 minutes)

Define what responsible use would require:

- Employee Training: Educate staff on ethical AI applications and transparency, including bias detection and privacy awareness
- Data Security: Use encryption and anonymization to protect data, aligning with global privacy standards
- Accuracy Verification: Fact-check AI outputs for reliability, using tools like retrieval-augmented generation
- Acceptable Use Policies: Define clear guidelines for AI usage, updated for 2025 trends
- Global Standards: Adhere to principles like UNESCO’s for fairness and human rights, alongside emerging EU AI Act guidelines
- Human Oversight: Ensure human review of critical AI outputs, especially in high-stakes applications.
- Sustainability Efforts: Minimize environmental impact through efficient AI practices, such as model optimization.
- Regular Audits: Conduct audits to ensure compliance with ethical standards and emerging regulations.

Agree on 3–4 safeguards you would put in place.
